================================================================================
DATA VERIFICATION
================================================================================

Before interpreting the graphs, several checks were run to confirm the data
is correct and consistent with publicly known facts about the Tor network.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHECK 1: Relay Count Per Day
------------------------------------------------------------------------
We know the Tor network has roughly 7,000–10,000 active relays at any
given moment (this has grown over recent years). We sampled several dates:

  2025-04-10 : 8,816 relays
  2025-07-01 : 8,661 relays
  2025-10-01 : 8,992 relays
  2026-01-01 : 9,476 relays

  Year average : ~9,157 relays/day
  Min (single day) : 8,372  on 2025-05-24
  Max (single day) : 10,052 on 2025-11-21

Result: PASS. These counts are consistent with the Tor Project's publicly
reported network size for this period.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHECK 2: Total Advertised Bandwidth vs. Tor Metrics
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Tor Project publishes aggregate bandwidth figures on metrics.torproject.org.
We computed total advertised bandwidth across all relays per day and converted
from kB/s to Gbit/s for comparison:

  2025-04-10 : 1,245 Gbit/s
  2025-07-01 : 1,220 Gbit/s
  2025-10-01 : 1,408 Gbit/s
  2026-01-01 : 1,668 Gbit/s
  Year average: ~1,451 Gbit/s

Result: PASS. These figures align with what Tor Metrics reports for advertised
network capacity in this period (~1.2–1.7 Tbit/s range).

------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHECK 3: Relay Bandwidth Distribution Sanity Check
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Median relay bandwidth : ~10,022 kB/s  (~10 MB/s)
  Mean relay bandwidth   : ~19,807 kB/s  (~20 MB/s)

The mean being roughly double the median confirms the network is skewed by
a small number of very high-capacity relays. This is consistent with the
well-known structure of the Tor network, where a handful of high-bandwidth
relays carry a disproportionate share of traffic.

Result: PASS. Distribution shape is as expected.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHECK 4: 26,399 Unique Relays — Is That Too Many?
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The 26,399 figure is not the number of relays active at once. It is the
total number of distinct relay fingerprints that appeared in at least one
daily snapshot across the full year. With ~9,000 relays active on any
given day and relays constantly joining and leaving the network, seeing
~26,000 unique relays over 365 days is reasonable and expected.

Result: PASS. This reflects normal network churn, not a data error.


================================================================================
GRAPH FINDINGS
================================================================================

All graphs use a 7-day rolling window. The solid line is the median value
across all relays in that group. The shaded band covers the 10th–90th
percentile range, showing how spread out behavior is across relays.

Relay groups are determined by each relay's median bandwidth over the full
year. Top 5% threshold: ≥64,000 kB/s. Top 10% threshold: ≥47,000 kB/s.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
ALL RELAYS — BANDWIDTH (all_relays_bandwidth.png)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The median relay offered approximately 10,000 kB/s (~10 MB/s) throughout
the year, with very little movement in the median line. This flatness
suggests the typical relay's capacity is remarkably stable over time.

The shaded band is extremely wide (roughly 0 to 60,000–70,000 kB/s),
reflecting the enormous diversity in the Tor relay ecosystem. Some relays
offer almost nothing; a few offer enormous capacity. The median sits low
because most relays are small — the high-capacity outliers pull the mean
up but do not affect the median.

Notable: There is a visible uptick in the upper percentile band between
October 2025 and January 2026, suggesting some high-capacity relays
joined or significantly increased their capacity during this period.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
ALL RELAYS — STD DEV (all_relays_stddev.png)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The median standard deviation sits around 700–800 kB/s for most of the
year, meaning the typical relay's bandwidth fluctuates by less than 1 MB/s
week-to-week in absolute terms. For a relay offering 10 MB/s, this is a
small variation.

The shaded band shows that some relays have much higher absolute fluctuation
(up to ~7,000 kB/s at the 90th percentile), but these are outliers.

Two spikes are visible:
  - September 2025: median std dev briefly reaches ~900 kB/s, with the
    90th percentile spiking to ~7,500 kB/s. This is the same event that
    appears across all graphs (see note below).
  - January 2026: a smaller secondary spike in the upper percentile band.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
ALL RELAYS — CV (all_relays_cv.png)  *** PRIMARY GRAPH ***
------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the most important graph for the project's goal.

The median CV sits at approximately 0.05 (5%) for most of the year. This
means the typical relay's bandwidth fluctuates by only about 5% of its
average value over any given 7-day window — an extremely stable network.

The shaded band (10th–90th percentile) ranges from near 0 up to ~0.20,
meaning most relays fall somewhere between essentially no variation and
20% variation. A small number of relays are much more volatile.

The most notable feature is the spike in September 2025, where the median
CV briefly reached ~0.35 (35% variation). This spike affects all relay
groups simultaneously, confirming it is a network-wide event rather than
isolated to a few relays.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
TOP 5% RELAYS — BANDWIDTH (top5_bandwidth.png)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The top 5% of relays (≥64,000 kB/s median) offered between roughly 75,000
and 100,000 kB/s over the year. There is a clear upward trend from April
2025 (~80,000 kB/s) peaking around October–November 2025 (~100,000 kB/s),
followed by a modest decline heading into early 2026.

The shaded band is wide but structured — the top and bottom of the band
move together, suggesting the whole group trends similarly. The dip at the
start of the dataset (April 2025) likely reflects that these relays were
still ramping up, or that the first few days of the rolling window have
fewer data points.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
TOP 5% RELAYS — STD DEV (top5_stddev.png)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Absolute fluctuation for the top 5% is much higher than the all-relay
median (~5,000 kB/s vs ~700 kB/s). This is expected — relays with more
bandwidth have larger absolute swings even if proportionally stable.

The September 2025 spike is more pronounced here, with the median briefly
reaching ~15,000 kB/s and the 90th percentile spiking to ~27,000 kB/s.
This suggests the September event disproportionately affected larger relays.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
TOP 5% RELAYS — CV (top5_cv.png)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Despite having much larger absolute fluctuations, the top 5% relays have
a median CV of ~0.05 — the same as the overall network. Larger relays are
proportionally just as stable as average relays.

The shaded band is noticeably narrower than the all-relays CV graph,
meaning the top 5% are more homogeneous in their stability behavior.
High-capacity relays tend to be run more consistently (likely by operators
with more infrastructure and resources).

The September 2025 spike reaches ~0.15 for the median (higher than the
all-relay spike of ~0.35 in absolute terms on the median, but note the
y-axes differ slightly). The spike is sharp and recovers within 2–3 weeks.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
TOP 10% RELAYS — BANDWIDTH / STD DEV / CV (top10_*.png)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The top 10% (≥47,000 kB/s) results sit between the all-relay and top 5%
figures, as expected. The bandwidth trend mirrors the top 5% but at lower
absolute values (~55,000–85,000 kB/s). Std dev is similarly intermediate.

The CV for the top 10% is nearly identical to the top 5% — both sit at
~0.05 median with similar band widths. The top 10% shows the same September
2025 spike and the same recovery pattern. This confirms the behavior of
high-capacity relays is consistent regardless of exactly where you draw
the threshold.


================================================================================
KEY FINDINGS
================================================================================

1. THE TOR NETWORK IS STABLE.
   The median CV of ~0.05 (5%) held consistently for most of the year
   across all relay groups. The typical relay does not fluctuate much.

2. HIGH-CAPACITY RELAYS ARE NOT MORE OR LESS STABLE THAN AVERAGE.
   Top 5% and top 10% relays have the same median CV as all relays. They
   just carry more traffic and have larger absolute swings.

3. THE SEPTEMBER 2025 EVENT IS THE MOST SIGNIFICANT ANOMALY.
   A clear spike appears simultaneously in CV, std dev, and bandwidth graphs
   across all three relay groups in September 2025. The median CV spiked to
   ~0.35 briefly. This is worth investigating further — possible causes
   include a major Tor software release, a directory authority change, a
   large relay or group of relays dropping off the network, or an external
   network event. Cross-referencing with Tor release history and Tor Metrics
   is recommended.

4. NETWORK CAPACITY GREW DURING THE PERIOD.
   Total advertised bandwidth increased from ~1,245 Gbit/s in April 2025 to
   ~1,668 Gbit/s by January 2026 — a ~34% increase in under a year. The
   number of relays also grew from ~8,800 to ~9,500 per day.

5. MOST OF THE 26,399 UNIQUE RELAYS ARE TRANSIENT.
   With ~9,000 active on any given day but 26,399 seen over the year, roughly
   two-thirds of relays that appeared during the year were not persistent.
   The 80% presence filter in relay_graphs.py ensures the analysis focuses
   on relays with meaningful, consistent participation.
