SEPTEMBER 2025 NETWORK EVENT — ANALYSIS
tor-relay-data | Dataset: 2025-04-10 to 2026-04-09 (strict intersection)
Date: 2026-04-10
================================================================================

OVERVIEW
--------
Every graph in this analysis shows a significant spike in bandwidth variability
during September 2025. This document summarizes what the data reveals about
the event. The root cause has not been confirmed — cross-referencing with
Tor Project release notes and Tor Metrics is recommended for further context.


================================================================================
TIMELINE
================================================================================

  Pre-event baseline (August 2025):
    Median CV: ~0.040–0.042  (very stable, slightly below the year average)

  Phase 1 — Mild onset (September 8, 2025):
    CV rose from ~0.037 to ~0.049
    Gradual, not a sharp break. Suggests something began changing on or
    just before September 8 that affected relay bandwidth incrementally.

  Phase 2 — Sharp spike (September 20, 2025):
    CV jumped from ~0.057 to ~0.085 within a single day.
    Continued rising to a peak of 0.101 on September 25.
    This is the sharpest single-week movement in the entire dataset.

  Peak: September 25, 2025
    Median CV: 0.101  (roughly 2.5× the normal baseline of ~0.040)

  Recovery: September 29–30, 2025
    CV fell back to ~0.048 within 4 days of the peak.
    Full recovery to baseline (~0.040) by mid-October.

  Post-event baseline (November 2025):
    Median CV: ~0.042  (identical to pre-event — no permanent change)


================================================================================
WHAT THE DATA SHOWS
================================================================================

1. TWO DISTINCT PHASES
   The event was not a single shock. There was a mild precursor starting
   September 8 (CV ~0.049) followed by a much sharper escalation on
   September 20. This two-phase structure suggests either two separate
   causes, or a single cause that had a delayed second-order effect.

2. BROADLY DISTRIBUTED ACROSS RELAYS
   On the peak day (September 25):
     - 1,092 of 2,153 relays (51%) had CV > 0.10
     - 434 of 2,153 relays (20%) had CV > 0.20

   The event affected the majority of the persistent relay set — this is
   not a handful of outliers skewing the aggregate. It was a network-wide
   phenomenon.

3. SMALL RELAYS WERE MORE AFFECTED
   Relays with CV > 0.20 during the peak had a median bandwidth of
   ~1,986 kB/s. Relays that remained stable had a median of ~14,714 kB/s.
   Smaller relays experienced proportionally more disruption than large ones.

4. BANDWIDTH WENT BOTH UP AND DOWN SIMULTANEOUSLY
   Comparing the week before the spike (Sep 1–7) to the peak week (Sep 20–26):
     - 1,025 relays increased bandwidth
     - 1,096 relays decreased bandwidth
     - Median change: -6 kB/s (nearly neutral in aggregate)
     - Mean change:  +233 kB/s (skewed by a few large gainers)

   This is important: the spike was not caused by the network uniformly
   gaining or losing capacity. Relays were moving in opposite directions
   at the same time, which is what drives CV up without necessarily changing
   the median bandwidth line much. This rules out a simple "big relay went
   offline" explanation.

5. FULL RECOVERY WITH NO PERMANENT EFFECT
   The post-event baseline (November 2025 onward) is identical to the
   pre-event baseline (~0.040–0.042). Whatever caused the disruption
   resolved completely and left no lasting change in network behavior.


================================================================================
POSSIBLE EXPLANATIONS
================================================================================

Based solely on the data characteristics above — two phases, broad impact,
small relays more affected, bidirectional bandwidth changes, full recovery —
the most likely categories of explanation are:

  A) Tor software release with a bandwidth-related change
     A new version of Tor that changed how relays report or measure bandwidth
     could cause a temporary period of adjustment while relays upgrade
     at different times. Staggered upgrades would produce bidirectional
     movement (upgraded relays change behavior, non-upgraded stay the same).
     This is consistent with the two-phase structure.

  B) Directory authority behavior change
     The Tor directory authorities assign consensus weights to relays. A
     change in how they measure or cap bandwidth could shift values across
     the network simultaneously. This would explain the broad impact.

  C) External network pressure
     A period of unusual traffic load or an attempt to stress the network
     could cause relays to fluctuate. However, the complete recovery with
     no baseline shift makes a sustained external cause less likely.

  D) Measurement artifact
     A change in how CollecTor records or publishes consensus data is
     unlikely given the event was confirmed across two independent collection
     methods (dump_consensus_panel.py and pull_relay_data.py).

================================================================================
CONCLUSION
================================================================================

The September 2025 event is real, confirmed across two independent datasets,
and represents the single largest disruption to relay bandwidth stability in
the April 2025 – April 2026 period studied. It affected over half of all
persistent relays, was bidirectional (not a simple capacity drop), and
resolved completely within 3–4 weeks with no lasting effect on the network.

The two-phase structure and the disproportionate impact on smaller relays are
the most diagnostic features for identifying the root cause. A Tor software
release with a bandwidth-related behavioral change remains the most likely
explanation pending external verification.
